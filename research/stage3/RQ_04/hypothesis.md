# Research Question

> "Can cache affinity improve reuse without violating per-class tail latency or fairness under skewed demand?" (stage3_candidates.md, RQ-4, line 196)

**Scope constraint (Stage 3A):** This stage establishes only whether the affinity-versus-tail/fairness problem exists as a **measurable phenomenon** under a matched harness. It designs no scheduler, admission policy, or fairness controller. The only experimental conditions permitted are three existing routing behaviors: **affinity-only** (greedy longest-prefix routing to the node holding the longest matching prefix), **load-only** (route to the least-loaded node, no prefix preference), and **simple threshold replication** (replicate the hot prefix to a fixed number of nodes when a popularity threshold is exceeded; otherwise affinity-only). All other system components are held identical across conditions.

# Motivation

Stage 2B retains RQ-4 as a Tier-1 validation question with an explicit gap verdict: gap G5 is classed "E — potential research gap" because "SGLang documents starvation; FastServe/Llumnix cover adjacent scheduler concerns without the prefix-affinity combination," with the instruction to "retain as RQ-4 with an explicit fairness metric" (stage2_final_review.md, G5 row). Section 7 item 4 compresses the research space around "cache locality versus per-class tail latency and fairness," and the Stage 2 outcome-reporting standard states that cache/routing studies must report "hit rate, queue delay, per-class/per-tenant slowdown or a declared fairness metric" (stage2_final_review.md, section 4 outcomes). The opportunity matrix marks cell 4 — scheduling × cache affinity × P95/P99 — as high-signal (A/A/A) with the instruction to "test whether saved prefill is outweighed by hotspot queueing or unfairness" (opportunity_matrix.md, cell 4). The local corpus motivates the phenomenon but does not measure it; the tension file T3 rates the trade-off's existence as Medium confidence and states the corpus "contains limited direct fairness metrics" (research_tensions.md, T3).

The mechanism side is documented. SGLang's RadixAttention uses greedy cache-aware longest-shared-prefix-first scheduling, which Theorem 3.1 proves is optimal (DFS) for hit rate only under cache size ≥ max request length, and the paper explicitly states the greedy policy "can cause starvation" and "may delay short requests," leaving integration with fair scheduling as future work (SGLang.md, §3, §8, Theorem 3.1). SGLang reports cache hit rates of 50–99% in benchmarks (96% of optimal on average) and production hit rates of 52.4% (LLaVA-NeXT-34B) and 74.1% (Vicuna-33B) with a 1.7× first-token latency reduction, while its note records **no P95/P99 metric at all** (SGLang.md, §6, §13: "median reported but p99 not shown"). The reuse opportunity is therefore real and large, and the mechanism that captures it is precisely the one the authors flag as starvation-prone.

The balancing side is also documented, with hot-key statistics. Mooncake's Conductor routes each request to the instance with shortest predicted TTFT = T_queue + T_prefill + T_transfer, with a manually tuned `kvcache_balancing_threshold` separating cache-aware prefill from transfer-based balancing; its own trace shows extreme block popularity skew — over 50% of blocks never reused while hot blocks are accessed tens of thousands of times — and its scheduling comparison (random vs load-balancing vs cache-aware vs full KVCache-centric) reports only **aggregate** average TTFT and SLO attainment, never per-class outcomes (Mooncake.md, §3, §4, §6, Fig 6, Fig 8). Mooncake's SLO itself is P90-based (TTFT_P90 ≤ 10× baseline, TBT_P90 ≤ 5×), one percentile looser than a strict P99 objective, and varying request priorities are explicitly listed as future work (Mooncake.md, §6, §12). Sarathi-Serve and FastServe bound tail behavior without any prefix affinity: Sarathi enforces P99 TBT via a fixed token budget (tau = 512 strict / 2048 relaxed, "5×/25× decode iteration" SLOs) and has **no prefix cache at all** (Sarathi-Serve.md, §6, §13); FastServe uses skip-join MLFQ with a starvation promotion threshold (alpha = 300 ms default), reports P95 tail latency and P95 goodput, measures queueing at up to 90% of end-to-end latency under long-tail loads, and also has no prefix-aware routing (FastServe.md, §3, §4, §6). Neither note contains any per-class fairness measurement.

KVFlow and Llumnix supply the concurrency and burst evidence. KVFlow's locality policies (workflow-aware eviction plus proactive prefetch) degrade under competing concurrency: at 1024-token fixed prompts and 64 concurrent workflows, the reactive hierarchical cache runs at **0.57× of GPU-only SGLang** — a documented concurrency collapse on the cache-affinity side — and the note's inferred limitation states client-ID disambiguation "does not address fairness/scheduling priority among clients; one client's prefetch may starve another," with no per-class measurement (KVFlow.md, §10, §13). Llumnix handles burst arrivals explicitly (Poisson and Gamma traces with varying coefficient of variation), attributes 70% of P99 degradation to preemption loss, and differentiates two priority classes (high-priority acceleration up to 1.5× with "normal requests performance preserved similar"), but uses FCFS within a class, states "no explicit discussion of fairness starvation," and has **no prefix sharing or KV reuse across requests** (Llumnix.md, §2, §6, §12, §13).

The measurement gap is therefore uniform across the corpus: **none of the six relevant notes reports a per-class/per-tenant slowdown metric or a declared fairness index.** SGLang documents starvation but does not measure it; Mooncake measures cache-aware scheduling only in the aggregate; Sarathi and FastServe manage tail without affinity; KVFlow and Llumnix observe concurrency/burst phenomena adjacent to the question without a fairness axis. Combined with assumption A3 (request independence — fragile: SGLang 50–99% wasted hit opportunities, RAGCache top 3% of documents receiving 60% of requests) and A6 (stationary Poisson workload — fragile under burst and skew), the local corpus supports the existence of the trade-off (T3, Medium) and justifies a matched-harness measurement of the joint hit-rate/tail/fairness frontier — exactly the RQ-4 "Cheap Validation" design: a skewed prefix trace, two request classes, three existing routing behaviors, and a declared per-class slowdown metric (stage3_candidates.md, RQ-4). No novelty is claimed; the corpus is cited only as motivation.

# Primary Hypothesis

Under prefix-popularity skew (hot-prefix request fraction p_hot ≥ 0.7) with hot and cold classes of equal mean total prompt length (2048 tokens; 50% shared prefix) served at equal total offered load within the stable region (hot-node utilization < 1.0), longest-prefix greedy (affinity-only) routing achieves a ≥G memory-accounted aggregate cache hit-rate gain (G derived in the E0 algebra step; expected ≥10 percentage points) and ≥20% lower aggregate mean TTFT than load-only routing, but the concentration of hot-class traffic on the hot node causes the hot class's P99 TTFT to exceed ≥2× its load-only value (hot-node queue hotspot), while the cold class's P99 TTFT stays within 1.2× of load-only.

This is negatable: if the hit-rate gain < G, or the hot-class P99 ratio < 2×, or the cold-class P99 ratio > 1.2× at the stated conditions, the hypothesis is false as stated.

# Sub-Hypotheses

**H1 — Hot-node queue hotspot exists despite aggregate improvement (tested at p_hot = 0.8, c = 32, stable load).** At skew p_hot = 0.8, concurrency c = 32, equal total offered load (within the stable region) and equal memory-accounted total KV budget, affinity-only routing achieves an aggregate cache hit rate ≥ G percentage points above load-only (G from E0, expected ≥10pp) and an aggregate mean TTFT ≤ 0.8× the load-only value, while the hot class's P99 TTFT under affinity-only is ≥2× the hot class's P99 TTFT under load-only, and the cold class's P99 TTFT under affinity-only is ≤1.2× its load-only value. H1 is the existence claim, on the mechanism the queueing model actually supports (hot-node concentration: M/G/1 analysis gives hot ratio ≥1.33 at any load, ≈2.7 at moderate load — design_review R1).

**H2 — The hotspot crosses a predeclared SLO at definable thresholds.** There exist a skew threshold p_hot* ∈ [0.7, 0.9] and a concurrency threshold c* ∈ [16, 64] such that, for all p_hot ≥ p_hot* and c ≥ c* (each dimension tested with the other fixed at its threshold), the hot class's P99 TTFT under affinity-only exceeds the common absolute SLO (P99 TTFT ≤ 2.0 s, revision R5) while the cold class's P99 TTFT under the same policy remains within the same SLO at ≥99% attainment. Additionally, p_hot* and c* are non-increasing in the fixed dimension. H2 converts H1's point observation into a boundary claim; the binding class is the hot class (the one the model predicts saturates first).

**H3 — The hot-class cost is attributable to the routing decision and controllable by replication.** At equal total offered load, equal memory-accounted cache budget, and equal request mix, the ratio (hot-class P99 TTFT under affinity-only) / (hot-class P99 TTFT under load-only) is ≥2× the corresponding cold-class ratio; and simple threshold replication of the hot prefix to two nodes (policy C) restores the hot class's P99 TTFT to within 30% of its load-only value while retaining aggregate hit rate within 5 percentage points of affinity-only. H3 establishes controllability: only the routing decision differs between conditions, so a hot-class regression that tracks the routing change is attributable to cache-affinity routing, and the threshold policy shows the phenomenon is routing-controllable rather than an unavoidable system property.

# Independent Variables

Minimal set (each swept at fixed values of the others):

1. **Skew** — hot-prefix request fraction p_hot ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} (revision R3: the Zipf decoration is dropped — it was arithmetically false; p_hot IS the skew parameter). The hot class's requests all share one common prefix of length L_prefix = 1024.
2. **Arrival pattern / burstiness** — Poisson arrivals at rate λ (three levels of offered load within the stable region, per-policy anchored, revision R4) and Gamma-burst arrivals with coefficient of variation CV ∈ {1, 2, 4} at matched mean rate (trace-replay: identical inter-arrival sequences replayed under every policy).
3. **Concurrency** — maximum in-flight requests c ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64}, enforced identically across conditions.
4. **Routing behavior** — the three existing policies as experimental conditions: affinity-only (greedy longest-prefix, tiebreak by token-weighted backlog — revision R6), load-only (least-loaded = minimum token-weighted backlog, no prefix preference), simple threshold replication (hot prefix replicated to k = 2 nodes when its request rate exceeds a fixed threshold; threshold held constant for the sweep).
5. **Request class definitions** — hot class (shared prefix + unique suffix) and cold class (unique, disjoint prefixes), equal mean total prompt length (2048, Uniform(1920, 2176)), class definitions fixed; skew varies only via p_hot (revision R3).
6. **Cache size** — total KV budget (memory-accounted: cache bytes per node × 4 nodes held EQUAL across policies, revision R2), at two levels: a level where the E0-derived affinity hit-rate gain G is expected to hold (calibrated per workload) and a level 50% smaller.

# Dependent Variables

Only what the hypotheses require, measured per class (hot, cold) and in aggregate:

1. **Cache hit rate** — fraction of prefill tokens served from cache, memory-accounted (H1's G leg; H3's 5pp retention clause).
2. **TTFT distribution** — mean, P50, P95, P99 per class (P99 drives H1/H2/H3; mean anchors the aggregate-improvement claim).
3. **Queue delay** — per-class mean and P99 time from arrival to first scheduled step (isolates the queue component of TTFT; distinguishes queue cost from prefill cost).
4. **Goodput under SLO** — per-class and aggregate fraction of completed requests with TTFT ≤ SLO (common absolute SLO, revision R5), and achieved RPS at ≥95% attainment.
5. **Declared per-class slowdown metric** — for class k and policy p: slowdown_k(p) = P99_TTFT(k, p) / P99_TTFT(k, load-only at equal offered load); plus the cross-class ratio construction in H3. **Declared fairness index FI** (Stage 2's missing metric, defined on the common absolute SLO, revision R5): FI(p) = min_k A_k(p) / max_k A_k(p) ∈ [0,1], where A_k(p) = per-class SLO attainment fraction on the common SLO; a policy is "fair on this index" only if FI(p) ≥ 0.95 and "unfair" if FI(p) < 0.8. **FI is a report-only metric** (revision R5 — the per-class verdicts ride on the direct P99 ratios, which H1–H3 specify).
6. **Replication traffic and memory** — for the threshold policy only: bytes transferred for replication, additional KV memory consumed.

# Control Variables

- **Model** — a single 7B decoder-only model (e.g., Llama-2-7B), fixed weights and FP16 precision; prefill/decode cost asymmetry thereby fixed across conditions.
- **GPU and topology** — fixed node count (4 GPUs) and interconnect; single-node placement so transfer cost ≈ 0 (the phenomenon under test is queueing, not transfer — Confounder 7 in the pre-revision version).
- **Framework and scheduler** — one engine (single code base); only the routing decision function varies; batch composition, chunked prefill, and batching policy identical.
- **Routing metric** — "least-loaded" = token-weighted backlog (pre-registered, revision R6); a request-count sensitivity cell is run and reported.
- **Total request count and total offered load** — fixed request count per run (10,000); identical inter-arrival sequence replayed across all policies at equal load (trace-replay). **Load anchoring (revision R4):** loads are fractions of the affinity configuration's own saturation, computed in E0; the overload region (hot-node utilization ≥ 1.0) is marked and excluded from all verdicts.
- **Seed, tokenizer, sampling** — fixed seed; deterministic greedy decoding; fixed max_tokens (256) per request.
- **Max batch / token budget** — fixed maximum tokens per batch (Sarathi-style tau = 1024), because batching aggressiveness independently moves the tail (T7).
- **SLO definition** — common absolute: per-class P99 TTFT ≤ 2.0 s (revision R5; the per-class 5×-isolated definition was abandoned — its denominators were 8× apart and not comparable).
- **Routing policy code path** — only the routing decision differs between conditions; all three policies share the same dispatch, admission, eviction, and execution code.

# Confounders

1. **Load imbalance / per-policy stability margins** — at a shared load, affinity operates closer to its own saturation than load-only (hot-node util = 2.67× per-node load-only util at the revised geometry). Detect: E0-computed utilization per cell; the overload region excluded from verdicts (revision R4). Neutralize: verdicts only within the stable region; per-policy saturation reported with every ratio.
2. **Batching/scheduler differences** — different routing changes which requests coexist in a batch (T7). Detect: batch-composition distributions (tokens-per-batch, class mix per batch) per condition; verify they do not differ beyond what the routing decision implies. Neutralize: identical scheduler code with only the router swapped.
3. **Hot-node CPU saturation** — radix tree and routing overhead live on the CPU. Detect: per-node CPU utilization; flag runs where any node exceeds 80%. Neutralize: node count chosen so routing work stays below the threshold in calibration.
4. **Cache size / budget effects** — hit-rate differences scale with cache size; the comparison is memory-accounted (equal total budget, revision R2). Detect: run at both budget levels; report all effects at both levels.
5. **Arrival process differences** — burstiness alone causes tail degradation (Llumnix: preemption loss = 70% of P99). Detect: run burst (Gamma CV) conditions under load-only and compare with affinity at the same trace. Neutralize: identical inter-arrival sequences across policies.
6. **Prompt-length asymmetry** — if hot prompts are longer than cold, prefill-cost asymmetry mimics or masks per-class effects. Restated per revision R6 as a **suffix-sensitivity test**: hot and cold classes have equal mean TOTAL prompt length; only the presence of the shared prefix (constitutive of the treatment) differs; the length-mismatched variant (hot mean 3072) and a suffix-length variant (hot suffix ∈ {256, 1024}) are run; an effect that scales with suffix length is a prefill-cost artifact, an effect that scales with p_hot (concentration) is routing-driven.
7. **Network / KV transfer contention** — replication traffic consumes bandwidth. Neutralize: single-node placement (transfer ≈ 0) for the primary experiment; replication bytes/memory measured explicitly for policy C (Confounders 7–8 of the pre-revision version retained).
8. **Replication memory pressure** — policy C consumes KV memory; total budget held constant across A/B/C (memory-accounted) and the comparison reported.
9. **Warm-up / cold-cache transients** — fixed warm-up schedule (hot prefix filled by a predeclared number of hot-class requests); results reported on the steady-state window only.

# Expected Observation If True

- **H1 true:** At p_hot = 0.8, c = 32, stable load (E0-anchored): hot-class P99 TTFT under affinity ≥ 2× its load-only value (M/G/1 prediction ≈ 2.7× at moderate load); cold-class P99 ≤ 1.2× load-only; aggregate mean TTFT ≤ 0.8× load-only; memory-accounted hit-rate gain ≥ G (expected ≥10pp); FI(affinity) < 0.8 while FI(load-only) ≥ 0.95 (common SLO, report-only).
- **H2 true:** The hot-class P99 TTFT curve under affinity crosses the common SLO at p_hot* ≤ 0.8 with c fixed at 32, and at c* ≤ 32 with p_hot fixed at 0.8; at p_hot = 0.9, c = 64 the hot class's attainment under affinity falls below 95% while the cold class's attainment stays ≥99% under the same policy and load.
- **H3 true:** The hot-class ratio P99(affinity-only)/P99(load-only) is ≥2× the cold-class ratio at equal total load; under simple threshold replication (2 replicas of the hot prefix), hot-class P99 TTFT returns to within 30% of load-only while aggregate hit rate stays within 5 points of affinity-only; replication traffic < 10% of total KV transfer.

# Falsification Condition

- **H1 falsified** if, at every tested skew level p_hot ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} and every concurrency c ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64} on Poisson arrivals at equal offered load within the stable region, the hot class's P99 TTFT under affinity-only stays within 20% of its load-only value; or if the memory-accounted hit-rate gain of affinity over load-only is ≤ 5 percentage points at all skew levels (no reuse advantage exists to trade off). A null on H1 reclassifies G5 as an engineering composition rather than a measurable tension, which is a valid Stage 3A outcome. (If E0 derives G < 10pp, the hit leg is killed pre-registrationally and H1 rests on the queue leg alone — revision R2.)
- **H2 falsified** if the hot class's P99 TTFT under affinity-only stays within the common SLO at ≥95% attainment or better across the entire tested range p_hot ∈ [0.5, 0.9], c ∈ [8, 128]; or if the identified thresholds fail the monotonicity condition (e.g., a regression at p_hot = 0.8 disappears at p_hot = 0.9), indicating non-monotone noise rather than a skew-scaling interaction.
- **H3 falsified** if the hot-class regression disappears under load-only routing at the same load (load-driven, not routing-driven); or if the hot-class ratio P99(affinity-only)/P99(load-only) equals the cold-class ratio within measurement error (±20%) at every tested condition; or if the effect scales with hot suffix length rather than with p_hot (suffix-sensitivity control, revision R6); or if simple threshold replication fails to move the hot-class P99 by more than 20% in either direction while holding hit rate within 5 points (the cost is not controllable through the routing decision).

# Ambiguous Outcome

The following outcomes would not discriminate between the hypotheses and would require a redesigned condition:

- **Both policies violate the SLO equally at high load:** the failure is load-limited (overload), not routing-limited; verdicts are drawn only within the stable region (revision R4) and the overload behavior is reported separately.
- **Hot and cold classes degrade together (hot-node saturation affecting everyone):** the observed effect is tail degradation from load concentration, not per-class unfairness; FI stays ≥0.95 and no per-class claim can be made. (Distinct from the pre-registered hot-only blowup, which IS the H1 prediction.)
- **Hit-rate differences too small for a queue trade-off to exist (gain between 6 and 14pp, or < G):** reported; the queue leg still decides H1 (revision R2).
- **Burstiness alone reproduces the full effect:** if Gamma-CV traces under load-only produce the same hot-class P99 as affinity-only does on Poisson traces, the tail harm is arrival-driven (Llumnix's documented preemption-loss regime), and the affinity attribution in H3 is untestable without a matched-trace design.
- **Framework-dependent results:** if the affinity-only regression appears under one engine's scheduler but not another's, the phenomenon is implementation-specific; the claim is bounded to the tested code path (T11).
- **Cold-class degradation appears on the real system but not in the simulator (E4 observation):** the cold-class effect requires within-node scheduling order (SGLang's documented longest-prefix-first starvation), which the FIFO simulator cannot represent; it is reported as a separate E4 observation, not merged into H1–H3 verdicts (revision R1, critic #D).

The deliverable of this stage is a binary, pre-registered verdict on H1–H3 from a matched harness; any "partial" outcome falls into one of the ambiguous categories above and must be reported as non-discriminating rather than as support.
